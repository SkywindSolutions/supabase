Getting Started

Your SkyWind Solutions dashboard is available at:

https://dashboard.skywindsolutions.com

Log in with the username and password provided by SkyWind Solutions. The dashboard opens automatically in kiosk mode — a full-screen view that shows only your forecast charts and controls, without any menus or sidebars. Depending on your account, you may have access to tide forecasts, visibility forecasts, or both.

After logging in, your default dashboard loads automatically. If your account has multiple dashboards (e.g., both tide and visibility), use the dashboard switcher at the top of the page to switch between them.

Navigating the Dashboard

All dashboards share common controls for selecting locations, adjusting the time window, and interacting with charts.

Choosing a Location

At the top of the dashboard, there is a Location dropdown. Click it to see the list of available locations for your account. Select a location to update all charts and readings on the page.

Changing the Time Range

In the upper-right corner of the dashboard, you will see the time range selector. It shows the current time window (for example, “now to now+7d” for tide, or “now to now+24h” for visibility).

To change the time range:

  1. Click the time range selector in the upper right.
  2. Choose a preset range from the Quick ranges list (e.g., “Last 6 hours”, “Last 24 hours”, “Last 7 days”), or set a custom range using the From and To fields.
  3. Click Apply time range to update the dashboard.
Tip: The forecast dashboards default to showing future data (e.g., “now to now+7d” for tide). If you set the time range to a past window, you will see historical forecast data for that period.

Zooming In and Out

You can zoom into any chart to see more detail:

  1. Zoom in: Click and drag across a section of any chart to zoom into that time period. Hold down the left mouse button and drag from left to right.
  2. Zoom out: To return to the original time range, click the Zoom out button (a small magnifying glass icon with a minus sign) at the top of the chart, or use the time range selector to reset the range.
  3. Reset zoom: You can also double-click on a chart to reset the zoom level.

Hovering Over Data Points

Move your mouse over any chart to see a tooltip with the exact value and timestamp for that data point. This is helpful for reading precise forecast values at a specific time.

Refreshing the Data

The dashboard refreshes automatically every 5 minutes. You can also refresh manually by clicking the refresh icon (circular arrow) next to the time range selector in the upper right.

Tide Forecasts

The tide forecast dashboard shows predicted water levels for the next 7 days.

Tide Forecast Chart

The main chart shows the predicted tide level over time as a continuous line. The horizontal axis is time, and the vertical axis is water level in feet.

  • The red line represents the forecasted water level at your selected location.
  • The shaded area represents the 90% confidence interval for forecasted water level.

Current Tide Reading

Below the chart, a large number displays the current tide reading in feet. This gives you an at-a-glance summary of the latest forecast value.

Tide Data Table

Below the chart, a data table lists the forecasted tide values with timestamps. You can use this to look up specific values or to compare readings across time. If your account has multiple locations, a separate “Tide by Location” table shows the latest reading for each location side by side.

Understanding Tide Values

  • Water levels are shown in feet relative to a standard reference level (datum).
  • Positive values indicate the water level is above the reference level.
  • Negative values indicate the water level is below the reference level.
  • Typical tidal cycles show two high tides and two low tides per day.

Visibility Forecasts

The visibility forecast dashboard shows predicted visibility conditions for the next 24 hours.

Visibility Forecast Chart

The main chart shows forecasted visibility over time. The horizontal axis is time, and the vertical axis is visibility distance in nautical miles.

  • Higher values on the chart mean better visibility (you can see farther).
  • Lower values mean reduced visibility — fog, haze, or other obscurations may be present.
  • Watch for periods where the line drops significantly, as these indicate times of poor visibility.

Probability of Reduced Visibility

A second chart shows the probability (likelihood) that visibility will be reduced at each forecast time. This is displayed as a percentage from 0% to 100%.

  • 0% means clear conditions are expected.
  • 100% means fog or restricted visibility is highly likely.
  • Use this chart alongside the visibility forecast to understand not just the predicted visibility, but how confident the forecast is.

Current Visibility Reading

A large number displays the current visibility value, giving you an instant summary of conditions right now.

Understanding Visibility Values

  • Visibility distance tells you how far you can see clearly.
  • Higher numbers = clearer conditions. A reading of 5+ miles typically means excellent visibility.
  • Lower numbers = reduced visibility. Readings below 1 mile often indicate fog, dense haze, or precipitation.
  • Pay special attention to times when both the visibility forecast drops and the probability of reduced visibility rises — these are the periods most likely to affect operations.

Users with Multiple Locations

If your account includes more than one monitoring location, you can switch between them using the Location dropdown at the top of the dashboard.

When you change the selected location:

  • All charts and readings on the page update to show data for that location.
  • The current reading panels update immediately.
  • Any data tables will reflect the newly selected location.

Comparing locations: To quickly compare conditions across your locations, look for the summary table panel at the bottom of the dashboard (labeled “Tide by Location” or “Visibility by Location”). This table shows the latest reading for every location in your account at once, without needing to switch between them.

Tip: If you need to monitor multiple locations simultaneously in more detail, open the dashboard in a second browser tab and select a different location in each tab.

Text Alerts (Tide Only)

Text alerts send SMS messages to your phone when tide levels are forecast to cross a threshold you set. At this time, text alerts are available for tide forecast locations only — visibility alerts are not yet supported.

Who Can Use Text Alerts

Text alerts are currently available to select users. If your account has access, you will see an “Alert Settings” button in the lower-right area of the dashboard when you are logged in.

If you do not see this button and believe you should have access, contact SkyWind Solutions support.

Setting Up a Text Alert

  1. Click the Alert Settings button in the lower right of the dashboard.
  2. A popup form will appear.
  1. Fill in the following fields:
    • Phone Number — Enter your mobile phone number in standard format (e.g., +1-555-123-4567). This is the number that will receive text messages.
    • Location — Select the tide monitoring location you want alerts for.
    • Alert Type — Choose Above or Below (see Alert Types below).
    • Threshold — Enter the water level (in feet) that should trigger the alert.
    • Cooldown — How long to wait before sending another alert for the same threshold crossing. Default is 1 hour.
    • Quiet Hours (optional) — Set a time window during which no alerts will be sent (see Quiet Hours below).
  2. Check the SMS consent checkbox. You must agree to the SMS terms before you can create an alert. Click the link to read the full terms if needed.
  3. Click Add Alert to create the alert.

Alert Types: Above and Below

  • Above: You will be alerted when the tide forecast shows water levels at or above your threshold. Use this if you are concerned about high water or flooding.
  • Below: You will be alerted when the tide forecast shows water levels at or below your threshold. Use this if you need to know about unusually low water levels (e.g., for navigation clearance).

Cooldown Period

After an alert is sent, the system waits for the cooldown period before sending another alert for the same subscription. This prevents you from receiving repeated messages about the same event. The default is 1 hour, but you can set it to any value that works for you.

Quiet Hours

If you do not want to receive alerts during certain hours (for example, overnight), set Quiet Hours:

  • Quiet Start — The time alerts should stop (e.g., 10:00 PM).
  • Quiet End — The time alerts should resume (e.g., 6:00 AM).

Quiet hours are evaluated in your browser time zone when the alert is created. Overnight windows that cross midnight are supported (e.g., 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM will suppress alerts from 10 PM through 6 AM).

Managing Your Alerts

To view, edit, or delete your existing alert subscriptions, open the Alert Settings panel the same way you created them. Your active subscriptions will be listed at the top. From there you can:

  • Enable or disable an alert without deleting it.
  • Delete an alert you no longer need.

Opting Out

You can stop receiving text alerts at any time in two ways:

  1. Reply STOP to any alert text message. This will opt you out of all SMS alerts.
  2. Disable or delete individual subscriptions through the Alert Settings panel on the dashboard.
Note: Standard messaging and data rates may apply.

Tips and Troubleshooting

  • Dashboard looks blank or shows “No data” — Check that a location is selected in the Location dropdown. If data is still missing, forecasts may not yet be available for that location or time period.
  • Charts are not updating — Click the refresh icon next to the time range selector. If the issue persists, try logging out and back in.
  • Time looks wrong — The dashboard uses your browser’s time zone by default. Check your computer’s clock and time zone settings.
  • Cannot find the Alerts button — Text alert access is limited to approved users. Contact SkyWind Solutions if you need access.
  • Not receiving text alerts — Verify your phone number is correct, your alert is enabled, and the current time is not within your quiet hours. Also confirm you have not previously replied STOP to a Skywind alert message.
  • Multiple browser tabs — You can open the same dashboard in multiple tabs with different locations selected to monitor several locations at once.

Glossary

TermDefinition
DatumThe reference level used to measure water height (e.g., Mean Sea Level).
CooldownThe minimum time between repeated alert messages for the same subscription.
ForecastA prediction of future conditions (tide levels, visibility) based on weather and ocean models.
High TideThe peak water level during a tidal cycle.
Low TideThe lowest water level during a tidal cycle.
Quiet HoursA time window during which text alerts are not sent.
ThresholdThe water level value that triggers an alert when crossed.
Time RangeThe period of time displayed on the dashboard charts.
TooltipA small popup that appears when you hover your mouse over a chart, showing the exact value at that point.
VisibilityHow far you can see clearly, affected by fog, haze, rain, and other conditions.
